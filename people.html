The people behind the project

A shared curiosity
about the stars.

CartographY brings together expertise in stellar physics, asteroseismology, statistical modelling and machine learning.

GD

Principal investigator

Guy Davies

Guy leads CartographY at the University of Birmingham. His research brings together asteroseismology, Bayesian inference and machine learning to understand stars and their populations.

University profile ↗

Research team

AS

Amalie Stokholm

Galactic archaeology

Developing hierarchical models of the Galactic thick disc, using asteroseismic constraints to investigate stellar populations.

VS

Victor See

Stellar rotation

Combining models of stellar rotation with asteroseismology and Bayesian methods to explore how stars change as they age.

EH

Emily Hatt

Asteroseismology & stellar clusters

Studying stellar interiors and using the shared properties of stars in clusters to constrain ages, masses and helium abundance.

GH

George Hookway

Stellar oscillations

Measuring individual oscillation frequencies in the K2 KEYSTONE sample and extending this work to binary systems.

Earlier team members & continuing collaborators

OH

Oliver Hall

Stellar rotation & Bayesian inference

Used asteroseismic rotation measurements and hierarchical Bayesian models to test how stars lose angular momentum as they age.

MN

Martin Nielsen

Asteroseismic methods & software

Developed automated methods for measuring stellar oscillations, including PBjam, and statistical tools for analysing large samples of stars.

TL

Tanda Li

Stellar modelling

Contributed stellar model grids, detailed modelling of red giants and methods for understanding model uncertainties.

AL

Alexander Lyttle

Hierarchical inference

Developed methods for modelling stars together and investigating the seismic signatures of helium.

OS

Owen Scutt

Neural network emulation

Developed neural networks that emulate stellar oscillation frequencies, enabling faster asteroseismic modelling.

Research areas describe contributions to CartographY during the project. The work also draws on a wider network of collaborators, acknowledged in our publications.
